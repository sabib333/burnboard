# BurnBoard Market Expansion

Repeatable, safety-aware, density-first expansion — not "launch everywhere at
once."

---

## 1. Market selection framework

Score candidate markets on: potential audience, creator ecosystem, language
readiness, competitive landscape, community density, cost of acquisition,
organic sharing potential, moderation readiness, local cultural fit, payment
readiness (where relevant). **Never expand merely because a country has a
large population.**

## 2. Market launch tiers

| Tier | Definition | Resource level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | Full priority market (density + creator supply likely) | Full localization, seeding, ambassadors, campaigns |
| Tier 2 | Targeted experiment (hypothesis-driven, bounded budget) | Light localization, community seeding, one playbook |
| Tier 3 | Organic availability (already localized language, no active campaign) | Product available; monitor only |

Spend is proportional to evidence, never uniform.

## 3. Market launch playbook

**Pre-launch:** creator recruitment, community seeding, localization
(language + cultural review), Trust & Safety readiness (language moderation
capability, reporting/appeals), content availability (liquidity check), soft
landing pages.

**Launch:** focused acquisition, ambassador activity, creator campaigns,
Challenges, Community events.

**Post-launch:** retention analysis, density measurement, safety analysis,
growth experiments. **Never scale paid acquisition before activation and
retention are acceptable** in that market.

## 4. Campus growth playbook (repeatable model)

ONE CAMPUS → DENSE USER CLUSTER → ACTIVE COMMUNITY → LOCAL CREATORS →
INVITATION LOOP → NEXT CAMPUS.

1. Pick a campus with a local interest community (university, city district).
2. Recruit 2–5 campus creators/ambassadors; seed a campus Community.
3. Run a Campus Challenge (native mechanic: challenges are invitation loops).
4. Measure density + activation + safety, document learnings.
5. Replicate to the next campus only after the loop demonstrably works.

## 5. City-by-city expansion

CITY SELECTION → LOCAL RESEARCH → CREATOR SEEDING → COMMUNITY SEEDING →
SOFT LAUNCH → DENSITY MEASUREMENT → EXPANSION. Judge markets by density and
retention cohorts, **not total signups**.

## 6. Community growth engine

Communities are local growth nodes: discovery (already in Explore/recommender),
invitations ("Join my Community" is a contextual invitation), sharing, and
activation. Seed communities around real interest groups (universities,
cities, creator audiences, professional groups, entertainment niches)
**before** large acquisition campaigns — never send users into empty
experiences.

## 7. Creator acquisition & ambassador programs

- **Segments:** emerging creators (strongest early network density), mid-tier,
  established, community leaders, niche experts. Never chase celebrities only.
- **Creator onboarding answers:** Why BurnBoard? Who is my audience? What can
  I create? How can I grow? What's different? — **no promises of instant
  followers, guaranteed virality, or guaranteed income.**
- **Ambassador framework:** early access, recognition, growth opportunities,
  community leadership, event participation. Measure quality, retention,
  community growth, and invited active users — never fake-engagement
  obligations. Local ambassadors get no unrestricted administrative power.

## 8. Challenge- and battle-driven growth

Challenges: PARTICIPATION → CONTENT → AUDIENCE → NEW PARTICIPANTS → MORE
CONTENT (native loop, already supported by `challenges`). Battles add
competition + audience participation + creator discovery — **Trust & Safety
rules stay fully active; nothing that rewards harassment.**

## 9. Safety readiness gates

Before aggressive acquisition into a market, verify: language moderation
capability, local abuse patterns, reporting flows, appeals, policy
compatibility. **Never launch growth faster than moderation capacity** — the
expansion sequence lists this as a hard gate, and growth accelerates
moderation load (more users → more content → more abuse opportunities → more
moderation).

## 10. Cultural product fit & local trust

Roast culture, humor, competition norms, privacy expectations and community
behavior differ by market. Localization must be cultural, not literal —
especially safety language (quality-reviewed translations). Local trust comes
from clear safety controls, transparent reporting, localized support where
justified and reliable moderation — **never hide safety weaknesses behind
growth marketing**.
